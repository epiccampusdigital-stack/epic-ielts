IELTS Timed Drills

Six short drills for Sunday's Final Exam. Press Start: the timer runs. Each answer locks as soon as you give it (typed answers lock when you press Enter or move on), so think before you choose. The drill is marked when you have answered every question or when time is up. Use headphones for Drill 1.

Drill 1 · Listening traps

In IELTS, speakers often change their answer: "sorry", "no, wait", "actually", "it's changed". Write ONLY the final answer. Each line can be played twice.

Drill 2 · Reading: TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN

TRUE = the text says the same thing in other words. FALSE = the text says the opposite. NOT GIVEN = the text does not say. Do not use your own knowledge.
--:--
Northport started a bike-sharing scheme in 2018. At first there were 500 bikes at 40 stations, but the scheme was so popular that the number of bikes had doubled within two years. Users pay 1,000 won for the first hour, and students pay half price. Not everyone was pleased. Some shop owners complained that the stations took up parking spaces outside their shops. The city responded by moving several stations to side streets. Today, the busiest station is outside the central railway station, where most trips are made between 7 and 9 in the morning. The city now plans to add electric bikes next year. These will cost more to use, but they will make it easier to ride up the steep hills in the north of the city. A survey found that 70 percent of users would like electric bikes, although only a third said they would pay more for them.

Drill 3 · Reading: matching headings

Read the FIRST and LAST sentence of each paragraph. Look for the main idea, not one word that matches. There are two extra headings.
--:--
A Many people believe that eating late at night causes weight gain. However, scientists say that the total amount of food matters more than the time it is eaten. A large meal at 7 p.m. has the same energy as the same meal at 10 p.m. B Even so, late meals can affect sleep. When the stomach is full, the body has to work hard to digest food, and people often wake up during the night. Doctors recommend finishing dinner at least two hours before going to bed. C Breakfast habits vary widely around the world. In Korea, many families eat rice and soup in the morning, while in Britain cereal or toast is more common. In Sri Lanka, hoppers or string hoppers are popular. D Some companies now help their workers eat better. They offer free fruit, healthy lunches and even cooking classes. Managers say that staff who eat well are more energetic and take fewer days off.

    The overview is the most important paragraph in Task 1. Write 2 sentences about the BIG trends only, with no numbers. Start with "Overall, ...". Use the table in Drill 4.

    Model overview Overall, the Science Museum became much more popular and was the most visited museum by 2025, while visitor numbers at the Art Gallery fell steadily. The History Museum's figures remained almost unchanged throughout the period. Check yours: Did you start with "Overall"? Did you mention all three museums? Did you avoid numbers?

    Drill 6 · Writing Task 2: plan and introduction

    Some people think that students should be allowed to use mobile phones in class. Others believe phones should be banned in schools. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
    5 minutes to plan, 10 minutes to write the introduction. Introduction = 1. say the topic in your own words, 2. "This essay will discuss both views", 3. your opinion.
    --:--
    0 words
    Model plan View 1: phones help learning: dictionaries, research, apps. View 2: phones distract students: games, social media, cheating. My opinion: allow them only when the teacher says so. Model introduction (52 words) Mobile phones have become part of everyday life, and many people now disagree about whether students should use them in class. This essay will discuss the arguments for allowing and banning phones in schools. In my opinion, phones can be useful learning tools, but only when teachers control how they are used.
